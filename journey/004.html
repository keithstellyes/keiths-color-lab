Every page so far has spent its bits the same way: an evenly spaced grid, whether that was levels of gray or a bit budget per channel. But no image uses the whole color cube. A photo of a forest is mostly greens, and an even grid still spends most of its entries on colors that photo does not contain anywhere.

So let's spend them somewhere else. A pixel no longer stores a color, it stores an index into a palette, and the palette gets chosen for the image in front of us. \(n\) bits buys \(2^n\) entries: 1 bit is two colors, 8 bits is the 256 of a GIF. The choosing is k-means, which drops \(2^n\) points into the image's colors and shuffles them around until each one sits in the middle of the colors nearest to it, and then every pixel is painted with whichever entry it ended up closest to. It runs on your GPU, so you can hand it a whole photo.

Same arrangement as before -- undithered on top, dithered below -- though the dither had to change. There is no fixed step size to add noise against anymore, so instead each pixel picks between the two palette entries it is closest to, in the ratio of how far it sits between them.
